1c:T70a,<p>We are excited to announce the submission of our first paper, <em>Beyond Borders: How Far Can We Predict African Crop Yields?</em>, to the Deep Learning Indaba 2026 (IJCAI Special Volume), AI for Social Impact track.</p>
<h2>What we built</h2>
<p>A multimodal benchmark for pan-African crop yield prediction covering 1,109 districts across 33 countries and 42 years of data (1981-2022). We combined 246 features from six different data sources:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>CHIRPS v2.0</strong>:precipitation statistics</li>
<li><strong>ERA5-Land</strong>:27 climate variables (temperature, radiation, soil moisture, wind)</li>
<li><strong>MODIS MOD13Q1</strong>:vegetation indices (NDVI, EVI, LSWI) with quality and cropland masking</li>
<li><strong>MODIS MCD64A1</strong>:burned area fraction</li>
<li><strong>Static features</strong>:topography (SRTM), soils (SoilGrids), land cover (ESA)</li>
<li><strong>Historical yields</strong>:lag values, rolling means, trends from HarvestStat Africa</li>
</ul>
<h2>Key result</h2>
<p>Our best model (XGBoost with Optuna hyperparameter tuning) achieves <strong>R² = 0.809</strong> and <strong>RMSE = 0.553 t/ha</strong> on the held-out test set (2019-2022), using a strictly temporal train/test split.</p>
<p>We also evaluated graph neural networks on a dual-edge graph connecting districts by geographic proximity and climate similarity, but found that an optimized XGBoost leaves no spatially structured residuals for a GNN to exploit.</p>
<h2>What's next</h2>
<p>We will present this work at DLI Lagos in August 2026. In the meantime, we are working on an interactive demo and exploring extensions to Sentinel-2 resolution and intra-season prediction.</p>
<p>All code and data are open source on <a href="https://github.com/beyond-borders-org">GitHub</a>.</p>
