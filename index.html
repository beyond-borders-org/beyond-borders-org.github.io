Open Research Initiative

Research questions that start where most datasets end

Beyond Borders works on problems that matter in African contexts, from children’s health to the languages people actually speak. Everything we produce is public: the code, the data we are allowed to share, and the reasoning behind each result.

Projects

Health Ongoing

Counting a child’s breaths, precisely enough to matter

In rural clinics, suspected pneumonia in a child is often assessed by counting breaths by eye for a full minute. NAFS measures breathing rate automatically on an ordinary smartphone, fully offline. It won a national hackathon, and a field pilot is underway in Morocco.

Speech and language In progress

What does a voice know that the written word forgets?

Many African languages are tonal: the same letters can mean different words depending on pitch. Written text often drops the marks that carry that difference. We are studying how much of it speech can give back.

How we work

Open by default

Code is released under the MIT license. Data and trained models are published whenever their licenses and the people behind them allow it.

Every number traceable

Each result we report comes with the notebook that produced it, so anyone can rerun it and reach the same figure.

Honest about limits

We report what did not work alongside what did. A negative result is still a result.

Work with us

Researchers, engineers, clinicians and speakers of the languages we study are all welcome. Tell us what you are working on.
