<h2>Methodology</h2>
<p>Features are extracted from Google Earth Engine at the district level, aggregated to annual statistics (mean, std, percentiles per growing season), and combined with static topographic, soil, and land cover variables. Historical yield features capture temporal patterns through lag values, rolling means, and trends.</p>
<p>We evaluate three approaches: (1) XGBoost with 246 hand-crafted features and Optuna hyperparameter optimization, (2) a GNN operating on a dual-edge graph connecting districts by geographic proximity and climate similarity, and (3) a hybrid that uses the GNN to model XGBoost residuals.</p>
<p>The temporal split ensures no future information leaks: train on 1981-2015, validate on 2016-2018, test on 2019-2022.</p>
<h2>Temporal Split</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Train:</strong> 1981-2015 (17,825 samples)</li>
<li><strong>Validation:</strong> 2016-2018 (2,061 samples)</li>
<li><strong>Test:</strong> 2019-2022 (1,628 samples)</li>
</ul>
<p>Strictly temporal. No random splitting.</p>
